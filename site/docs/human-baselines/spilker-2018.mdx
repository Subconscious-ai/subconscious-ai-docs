## Same answers, or the same causes?

<p className="hb-cap">A conjoint randomizes every attribute, so the effects on this page are already interventional, not correlational: matching them is more than matching marginals. What that does not settle is whether the <em>structure</em> of causes matches. These are the measures that ask, one rung at a time. Each is drawn only where the data supports it.</p>

<div className="hb-ladder"><div className="hb-rung"><div className="hb-r"><b>Association</b>Rung 1</div><div className="hb-a">Do synthetic respondents answer the way people answer?</div><div className="hb-m">Rank correlation of the effects, RMSE after one fitted scale, accuracy against the human ceiling. Necessary, and where most of the industry stops.</div></div><div className="hb-rung"><div className="hb-r"><b>Intervention</b>Rung 2</div><div className="hb-a">When the experimenter changes an attribute, does the effect on choice match in size, direction and shape?</div><div className="hb-m">A conjoint randomizes every attribute, so its effects are already interventional. Calibration slope, equivalence level by level, dose-response, and the interaction fingerprint live here.</div></div><div className="hb-rung"><div className="hb-r"><b>Heterogeneity</b>Rung 2½</div><div className="hb-a">Is the effect larger for the same kind of person?</div><div className="hb-m">Conditional effects by respondent stratum. The literature&#x27;s weak point: main effects replicate at about .85 while treatment-by-subgroup interactions replicate at about zero.</div></div><div className="hb-rung"><div className="hb-r"><b>Counterfactual</b>Rung 3</div><div className="hb-a">For this respondent, would one changed attribute have flipped the choice?</div><div className="hb-m">The spread of within-respondent effects, against the human spread. Averages can agree while the spreads do not.</div></div></div>

<div className="hb-stats hb-mech">

<div className="hb-deck">Slope says whether the panel feels causes as strongly as people; dispersion whether it is more certain than people; equivalence how many levels agree inside a margin we state rather than derive.</div>

### Dose-response on ordered attributes

<section className="hb-fig">
<div className="hb-fighead"><p className="hb-figtitle">As the dose rises, do both sides move the same way?</p><div className="hb-hint">Solid: human · dashed: synthetic · τ compares effect order with level order</div></div>
<div className="hb-wrap"><Dose /></div>

### Which attributes interact

<section className="hb-fig">
<div className="hb-fighead"><p className="hb-figtitle">Every pair of attributes, scored against shuffled answers.</p><div className="hb-hint">Darker is a stronger interaction · z above 3 is beyond chance</div></div>
<div className="hb-wrap"><Fingerprint /></div>
<p className="hb-cap">An interaction is two attributes that matter more, or less, together than apart, and it is the part of a mechanism a rank correlation cannot see. The panel shows 4 pairs beyond chance. This paper has no released respondent data, so only the synthetic side is drawn. <em>Raw variance explained grows with the number of levels a pair adds, so two shuffled copies of the same data would "agree" at about .86 on it; only the null-calibrated score is drawn.</em></p>
<div className="hb-src"><b>Source</b> respondent-level answers for the synthetic side &nbsp; <b>Method</b> ΔR² of each pairwise interaction over the additive model, z against 25 shuffles of the outcome &nbsp; <b>Status</b> <span className="hb-st">exploratory</span></div>
</section>

### Would it have changed this respondent's mind?

<section className="hb-fig">
<div className="hb-fighead"><p className="hb-figtitle">One effect per respondent, not one effect per study.</p><div className="hb-hint">Synthetic only: this paper released no respondent data</div></div>
<div className="hb-wrap"><Counterfactual /></div>
<p className="hb-cap">Each respondent saw the same contrast more than once, so each has an effect of their own: the share they chose when <b>Employment in the manufacturing sector will increase</b> was present, minus the share when it was not.  A panel that answers with one voice would pile up on a single value where people spread out, and 24% of these respondents move against the average effect. <em>These are within-respondent contrasts inferred from separate tasks, not paired counterfactual tasks; asking the same respondent the flipped profile is the next step.</em></p>
<div className="hb-src"><b>Source</b> respondent-level answers for the synthetic panel &nbsp; <b>Method</b> share chosen with the level minus share chosen without it, per respondent &nbsp; <b>Status</b> <span className="hb-st">rung-3 evidence by inference, not by paired design</span></div>
</section>
